Delegation

50 Tasks You Can Hand Off to a VA This Week

Use this practical list of 50 tasks to identify what a virtual assistant can take off your plate across admin, marketing, operations, and support.

By VIA VA Team  ·  6 min read

Delegation becomes easier when you stop asking, ‘What can a VA do?’ and start asking, ‘What should no longer require me?’ A virtual assistant can take ownership of recurring work, maintain the systems behind your business, and protect time for decisions only you can make. You do not need to hand off all 50 tasks at once. Circle the work that is repetitive, teachable, and currently slowing you down, then begin with a small, clear group.

Inbox and calendar

  • Sort and label incoming email
  • Draft routine replies
  • Flag messages requiring your decision
  • Send follow-up reminders
  • Manage appointment scheduling
  • Confirm meetings and time zones
  • Protect focus blocks
  • Prepare a daily calendar brief

Treat these as responsibilities rather than isolated requests. Define the frequency, tools, source material, approval point, and completion standard for the group. This gives a VA enough context to manage the workflow consistently and spot problems early.

Client and customer support

  • Answer common questions from approved templates
  • Send onboarding instructions
  • Collect missing forms and assets
  • Update client records
  • Schedule check-ins
  • Track open requests
  • Prepare status updates
  • Send satisfaction surveys

Operations and administration

  • Organize cloud folders
  • Rename and file documents
  • Maintain SOPs
  • Update project boards
  • Create meeting agendas
  • Capture notes and action items
  • Track deadlines
  • Prepare weekly reports
  • Enter expenses
  • Follow up on invoices

Marketing support

  • Schedule approved social posts
  • Format blog posts
  • Upload images and add alt text
  • Repurpose long-form content
  • Build email newsletters
  • Maintain a content calendar
  • Research content ideas
  • Collect testimonials
  • Update website copy
  • Prepare simple Canva graphics

Sales support

  • Research prospects
  • Clean lead lists
  • Update CRM stages
  • Log call notes
  • Send approved follow-ups
  • Schedule discovery calls
  • Prepare proposal details
  • Track pipeline activity

Real estate support

  • Coordinate listing documents
  • Organize property photos
  • Schedule showings
  • Prepare listing updates
  • Follow up with agents using approved scripts
  • Maintain transaction checklists
  • Create neighborhood research packets
  • Draft social content for listings

How to put this list into practice

The best first delegation is not necessarily the task you dislike most. Choose work with a clear trigger, repeatable steps, accessible tools, and an objective finish line. For example, ‘manage my inbox’ is too broad. ‘Every weekday at 9:00 a.m., label newsletters, archive receipts, flag client messages, and draft replies for approval’ is trainable. Start with three to five related tasks, provide examples, and agree on how questions should be escalated. Review the process after one week, improve the instructions, and gradually increase ownership. Effective delegation is a system: context, standards, access, communication, and feedback all matter. When those pieces are in place, your VA does more than complete tasks—they create continuity.

A simple implementation plan

  • Track your work for three days without trying to change it.
  • Mark each task as only-me, delegate, automate, or eliminate.
  • Choose three related tasks with low risk and high frequency.
  • Record a short walkthrough and provide one strong example.
  • Set a check-in rhythm and a clear definition of done.

Keep the first version intentionally small. Review what happened, capture questions, improve the instructions, and repeat. Sustainable capacity comes from a dependable process, not from handing over a large pile of work in a single day.

Common mistakes to avoid

Avoid unclear requests, missing deadlines, incomplete access, and silent assumptions about quality. Do not measure success only by activity; look for a reliable outcome, fewer dropped details, and more time available for higher-value work. When something goes wrong, examine the process, training, and decision boundaries before adding more supervision.

Make the change sustainable

Begin with a baseline instead of relying on how busy the work feels. Note how long the current workflow takes, how often it is delayed, how many corrections it needs, and what higher-value work is displaced. A baseline gives you something concrete to improve. After two to four weeks, compare the same measures. The result may be time saved, faster response, fewer missing details, more consistent output, or simply a calmer week. Each of those is more useful than counting how many individual tasks were completed.

Ownership should be specific. One person needs to know when the workflow starts, what information is required, which result they are responsible for, and when to ask for help. This does not mean the owner must personally perform every step. It means the process no longer depends on everyone assuming someone else will notice. A named backup is also helpful for client-facing or deadline-sensitive work, especially when the primary owner is unavailable.

Build the workflow around exceptions, not only the ideal example. Ask what happens when a form is incomplete, a client does not respond, an approval is late, a tool is unavailable, or the request falls outside the usual scope. You do not need a rule for every imaginable situation. Cover the common exceptions and create a simple escalation path for everything else. This lets the work continue without encouraging risky guesses.

Communication should be designed into the process. Decide what needs an immediate message, what belongs in the project system, and what can wait for a scheduled update. A concise report might include completed work, upcoming deadlines, blockers, questions, and suggested improvements. This gives the business owner visibility without requiring constant check-ins. It also gives the person doing the work a predictable place to surface issues and demonstrate progress.

Frequently asked questions

How many tasks should I delegate first?

Three to five related tasks are usually enough for a focused first week. Add more after the workflow is stable.

What if explaining the work takes longer than doing it?

The first handoff is an investment. Record the process once, let your VA turn it into an SOP, and improve it through real use.

Can a VA make decisions?

Yes, within agreed boundaries. Define what they may decide, what needs approval, and what must be escalated immediately.
